A premium minivan — for family or for business, your call

The Alphard is equally good in both roles. For a family: room for everyone, captain's chairs and quiet on long trips. For business and passenger transport: a Lexus-level cabin, silence and the kind of comfort clients come back for.

And all at an affordable price: most of our cars are up to $15,000 cleared through customs in Ukraine. If you want something richer, we also have newer 2016–2017 cars.

The car Japan uses to chauffeur its VIPs

In Japan, nobody drives an Alphard "just because". It meets guests at five-star hotels and carries company executives and celebrities. Outside it's a minivan, but inside you step into a cabin that feels more like a limousine or a business-class seat on a plane. The design and trim are at Lexus level: soft leather, silence, light that is easy on the eyes.

Vellfire — its bold twin

The same car with a sharper face: split headlights, crisp lines, an aggressive grille. Toyota built the Vellfire mainly for the Japanese market and barely sold it officially anywhere else. So every Vellfire you see outside Japan came from a Japanese auction — just like ours.

The second row people buy it for

The Alphard also comes as an eight-seater, with a three-seat bench in the second row. But we mostly bring seven-seaters: with two captain's chairs the cabin is richer and more comfortable. The chairs have extending footrests. Recline the back, raise your legs — and a five-hour drive becomes a rest. There's a walkway between the chairs to the third row. Three more seats in the back. With all seats up the boot is small, but fold the third row and it takes even a house move. The side doors open at the push of a button — priceless when your hands are full of shopping bags.

In the evening the cabin glows with soft contour lighting — ambient light, as in expensive sedans. Both the 20 and the 30 series have it.

Big, yet easy to handle

Almost five metres long, yet it moves through the city with ease. Tested personally: it fits into ordinary car parking spaces without trouble. The high seating position and huge windows give you a view you quickly get used to — and miss in other cars.

20 or 30 series

  • 20 series (2008–2014) — the classic. 2.4 engine, automatic, proven mechanics, many parts shared with the Camry. The smartest balance of price and comfort.
  • 30 series (from 2015) — the new generation: big chrome grille, modern cabin, 2.5 engine. It costs more, but it looks like it arrived last and is still the boss.

Honestly about fuel

It's a big, heavy car: 10–12 litres of petrol in the city. But LPG fits the 2.4 and 2.5 engines without problems — and trips become noticeably cheaper.

About the steering wheel

Japanese cars are right-hand drive. Our Alphards are converted to left-hand drive in Georgia: the steering column, pedals and dashboard are moved. The stalks move too, so the indicators and wipers feel unusual for the first few days — then your hand gets used to it. We showed how the conversion works in detail in a separate article.
